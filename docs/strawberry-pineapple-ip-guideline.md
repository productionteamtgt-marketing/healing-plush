# Strawberry & Pineapple IP Guideline

> Status: Living document  
> Primary characters: Strawberry and Pineapple  
> Primary platforms: Instagram and YouTube  
> Last updated: 2026-09-22

## 1. Purpose

This document is the source of truth for the Strawberry and Pineapple social-media IP. Use it before developing character artwork, scripts, storyboards, GIFs, memes, short videos, or long-form episodes.

The guideline should remain stable at the level of character identity and brand promise. Individual stories may introduce new locations, costumes, props, and emotions as long as they do not contradict the rules below.

## 2. Current decisions

### Locked

- Strawberry and Pineapple are the two primary characters.
- Strawberry is female.
- Their core relationship is coworkers and friends.
- They do not live together. Each has a separate home, personal routine, private life, and individual preferences.
- They mainly meet at work and occasionally choose to spend time together outside work.
- The emotional tone is comforting, relatable, gently funny, and grounded in adult life.
- Instagram attracts and engages the audience through images, GIFs, memes, and short videos.
- Most Instagram videos use visual acting, music, ambience, and sound effects without spoken dialogue, human voices, or narration.
- YouTube is the main home for longer stories, audience retention, and monetization.
- Visual identity follows the established healing-plush character style and canonical character references.

### To be decided

- Final account and IP name
- Final English and Chinese character names
- Primary language and subtitle strategy
- Each character's specific hobbies, favorite foods, home style, and solo routines
- Upload schedule and production cadence
- Whether romance will remain permanently ambiguous
- Whether any future YouTube format will use narration or spoken voices

Do not silently convert a `To be decided` item into canon. Record the decision here when it is approved.

## 3. Core positioning

### One-line positioning

**Two tiny coworkers trying their best at being adults.**

### Chinese expression

**两个小小上班族，努力应付成年人的生活。**

### Brand promise

The characters turn ordinary adult pressures into small, warm stories that make viewers feel seen, understood, and less alone.

The work is not merely about cute fruit characters. Their softness creates contrast with recognizable experiences such as work stress, overthinking, being late, low energy, money worries, friendship, food, and the need for small comforts.

### Emotional statement

**Adulting is easier when you are not doing it alone.**

Chinese interpretation:

**生活没有变容易，只是有人陪你一起。**

## 4. Audience

Primary audience:

- Young adults navigating work, relationships, money, fatigue, and everyday uncertainty
- Viewers who enjoy cozy characters but want emotional truth rather than empty cuteness
- People likely to share content with a friend using language such as “this is us” or “this is literally me”

The stories should remain accessible internationally. Emotional situations must be understandable without requiring knowledge of a specific workplace culture or internet trend.

## 5. Character profiles

### Strawberry

#### Role

The emotional observer and inner narrator of the duo.

#### Personality

- Gentle, sensitive, observant, and outwardly composed
- Slightly perfectionistic and prone to overthinking
- Notices subtle changes in other people's expressions and behavior
- Enjoys office gossip as a way of understanding relationships, not as cruelty
- Tries to maintain harmony and may hide her own discomfort
- Has taste, rituals, and an appreciation for small beautiful things

#### MBTI writing model: INFJ

- **Introverted:** comfortable with trusted people; social situations drain her energy.
- **Intuitive:** reads between the lines and imagines many possible meanings.
- **Feeling:** makes decisions with strong awareness of emotional impact.
- **Judging:** prefers preparation and becomes anxious when plans lose structure.

#### Core contradiction

She understands and cares for everyone else's feelings, but often forgets to care for herself.

#### Comedy engine

- Turns one short message from the boss into ten possible interpretations
- Says “I'm fine” while visibly replaying an event in her head
- Creates a perfect plan and delays starting because it may not be perfect enough
- Becomes extremely polite when she is actually annoyed

#### Emotional need

Permission to stop managing everything and be cared for without having to ask perfectly.

#### Internal writing voice

> “I'm not overthinking. I'm just thinking about everything.”

This is a guide for captions, titles, memes, and story intent. It is not normally spoken aloud in Instagram videos.

#### Performance notes

- Reactions are small but readable: a pause, lowered gaze, tiny sigh, frozen smile, or slow blink.
- Her humor is dry, observant, and sometimes expressed through internal monologue.
- Do not write her as helpless, constantly sad, or fragile. Her sensitivity is also intelligence and strength.

### Pineapple

#### Role

The present-minded action and physical-comedy engine of the duo.

#### Personality

- Sincere, warm, energetic, and slightly slow to notice social signals
- Serious about helping, even when his method is improvised
- Easily distracted and frequently running a little late
- Does not always know the right words, so he shows care through action
- Finds simple reasons to be happy and helps Strawberry return to the present
- Can deliver an absurd conclusion with complete confidence

#### MBTI writing model: ESFP

- **Extraverted:** seeks interaction and creates conversation when things become too quiet.
- **Sensing:** responds to what is happening now instead of imagining every future outcome.
- **Feeling:** prioritizes people and loyalty even when he cannot explain his emotions well.
- **Perceiving:** adapts in the moment, improvises, and often leaves things until the last minute.

#### Core contradiction

He tries to make everyone around him feel better but may not notice when he is also exhausted.

#### Comedy engine

- Stops for breakfast even though he is already late
- Replies before realizing he misunderstood the question
- Starts solving a problem before checking whether it is the correct problem
- Is still holding bubble tea when everyone else has started pretending to work

#### Emotional need

Permission to slow down and admit that optimism does not mean he is always okay.

#### Internal writing voice

> “I don't have a plan, but I brought snacks.”

This is a guide for captions, titles, memes, and story intent. It is not normally spoken aloud in Instagram videos.

#### Performance notes

- Uses larger, clearer actions than Strawberry: quick turns, little runs, surprised freezes, and enthusiastic gestures.
- His humor comes from timing, literal interpretation, and confident improvisation.
- Do not write him as stupid, careless, or only comic relief. His emotional intelligence appears through practical kindness.

## 6. Character relationship

### Canon relationship

Coworkers and friends.

They work together and know each other's habits well enough for small gestures and unspoken signals to matter. They do not share a home and should not be portrayed as automatically spending every morning, evening, or weekend together. Each character has an independent life beyond the duo.

They occasionally arrange meals, shopping trips, day trips, celebrations, or casual outings together. These moments feel chosen and special because they are friends, not because they live together. Romance is not currently canon. Their warmth may remain open to audience interpretation without turning every interaction into romantic coding.

### Mutual effect

**She helps him think further ahead; he helps her live in the present.**

### Interaction pattern

| Situation | Strawberry | Pineapple |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Running late | Prepares multiple explanations | Notices the time after arriving |
| Boss sends a message | Analyzes every punctuation mark | Replies with the first suitable emoji |
| The other is upset | Observes carefully and hesitates to ask | Quietly brings a drink or snack |
| Weekend plans | Creates a complete schedule | Suggests a different destination halfway there |
| A problem appears | Thinks until action feels risky | Acts before confirming the method |
| They are slacking off | Worries about being caught | Temporarily forgets the boss exists |

### Relationship guardrails

- Conflict should emerge from different coping styles, not cruelty.
- Teasing may be affectionate but must not humiliate either character.
- Both characters must sometimes give and receive care.
- Avoid making Strawberry the permanent responsible parent and Pineapple the permanent incompetent child.
- Their friendship should feel reciprocal, safe, and emotionally earned.
- Preserve personal boundaries: they may go home separately, decline an invitation, enjoy different activities, or have an experience without the other character.
- Not every story requires both characters. Solo stories should deepen the individual's habits and preferences while preserving the same shared world.

## 7. Story world

Primary recurring spaces:

- Office and commute
- Strawberry's home and personal routines
- Pineapple's separate home and personal routines
- Café, convenience store, supermarket, and lunch locations
- Shared outings such as cafés, parks, shopping streets, day trips, and seasonal events
- Small city environments such as elevators, bus stops, sidewalks, and rainy windows

The world should feel warm and tactile even when the subject is stressful. Everyday objects can become part of the comedy, but they must not overwhelm the characters.

Their homes must not be visually interchangeable. Once each character's home style and hobbies are approved, record them here and maintain continuity. Do not imply that one routinely sleeps or lives at the other's home.

## 8. Story engine

### Default episode formula

1. A recognizable adult-life pressure appears.
2. One character's coping style makes the situation funnier or more complicated.
3. The two characters interact, misunderstand, or attempt a solution.
4. A small act of care changes the emotional direction.
5. End on a warm image, gentle visual joke, or caption-ready idea.

Short form may compress these beats, but it should still provide a complete emotional payoff rather than functioning only as an advertisement.

For dialogue-free video, replace verbal explanation with a clear visual chain: setup object or situation, readable reaction, physical attempt, consequence, and emotional resolution.

### Content pillars

1. **Work survival** — Mondays, meetings, bosses, deadlines, commuting, pretending to be productive
2. **Inner life** — overthinking, low energy, comparison, imposter feelings, needing rest
3. **Friendship and care** — food, listening, quiet companionship, noticing small needs
4. **Adulting comedy** — budgeting, healthy-living attempts, chores, forgotten plans, late arrivals
5. **Tiny comforts** — sunlight, rain, snacks, warm drinks, going home, finishing one difficult task

### Example premises

- They pretend the elevator is broken because neither is ready for Monday.
- Salary arrives; after paying everything, they celebrate having enough left for bubble tea.
- Strawberry says she is fine; Pineapple silently leaves the last piece of cake for her.
- Both work intensely when the boss walks past, then resume gossip the moment the boss leaves.
- They plan a healthy dinner and order fried food ten minutes later.
- Pineapple runs to work in a panic and discovers Strawberry arrived at the same time.
- A terrible day ends with the two of them watching an unexpectedly beautiful sunset.

## 9. Tone and writing rules

### Desired tone

- Warm, restrained, and emotionally observant
- Cute but not infantile
- Funny without becoming loud or chaotic all the time
- Relatable without relying only on negativity
- Hopeful without pretending real problems disappear instantly

### Silent visual storytelling

- Instagram video is visual-first and normally contains no spoken dialogue, human voice, or narrator.
- Tell the story through eye direction, pauses, posture, paw gestures, prop interaction, entrances and exits, reaction timing, and clear cause and effect.
- Use music, room ambience, footsteps, fabric movement, object sounds, notification sounds, cooking sounds, and comic sound effects to support the action.
- The story must remain understandable when muted; sound adds rhythm and emotion but should not carry essential plot information by itself.
- Written language may appear in post captions, meme images, titles, descriptions, or occasional essential interface text. Keep in-video text minimal and internationally understandable.
- Character thoughts and sample lines in this guideline describe writing intent. They are not default spoken dialogue.
- If a future production uses nonverbal character noises, keep them abstract and toy-like rather than human speech, and approve the sound language before making it recurring canon.

### Avoid

- Generic motivational speeches
- Constant crying, misery, or workplace complaining without payoff
- Characters behaving cruelly for a joke
- Copying another creator's plots, dialogue, framing, or character dynamics
- Trend participation that contradicts the established personalities
- Turning every warm interaction into explicit romance
- Exposition delivered through voice-over because the visual action is unclear
- Lip-synced human speech or dialogue-heavy scenes in the default Instagram format

## 10. Platform roles

### Instagram

Instagram is the discovery, sharing, and relationship-building platform.

Its default moving-image language is dialogue-free character performance: visual comedy, expressive pauses, music, ambience, and sound effects. The approach may draw inspiration from the accessibility of classic character brands and dialogue-light comedy, including Sanrio characters and Gudetama, while keeping this IP's character designs, situations, timing, and emotional identity original.

Recommended content mix:

- **40% images, carousel posts, and relatable memes**
- **30% Reels and short narrative videos**
- **15% GIFs, reaction loops, and sticker-like expressions**
- **15% episode previews, character moments, and behind-the-scenes material**

Every Instagram post should deliver its own emotion or joke. Calls to action should invite viewers to continue a story on YouTube after receiving value, rather than withholding the entire payoff.

For Reels and GIFs:

- Start with an immediately readable pose, object, or problem.
- Use strong silhouettes and distinct Strawberry/Pineapple reaction timing.
- Build loops around a repeated action, reaction, or small failure.
- Avoid relying on mouth movement or subtitles to explain the scene.
- Design the final frame so it can either loop cleanly or become a strong still image.

Useful share behavior to design for:

- “This is me.”
- “This is us.”
- “Sending this to my work bestie.”
- “I needed this today.”

### YouTube

YouTube is the primary home for complete stories, watch time, deeper attachment, and monetization.

Long-form episodes should retain the same visual-first identity. Music, ambience, foley, and sound design can carry longer emotional arcs. Spoken narration or character voices are not currently established canon and remain a future format decision.

Recommended content layers:

1. **Shorts:** vertical discoveries adapted from short narrative moments
2. **Regular episodes:** approximately 4–8 minutes, built around one theme or several connected incidents
3. **Themed compilations:** approximately 15–25 minutes, reorganized around a satisfying emotional theme

Do not pad an idea merely to reach a target duration. Combine related mini-stories when a single premise cannot sustain a longer episode.

## 11. Instagram-to-YouTube funnel

1. Introduce a recognizable problem visually and quickly on Instagram.
2. Deliver a complete joke or emotional beat.
3. Reveal through action or the final visual beat that the situation continues or has a larger consequence.
4. Direct viewers to a specifically named YouTube episode.
5. Keep profile links, pinned posts, titles, thumbnails, and episode naming consistent.

Example:

> She thought Monday was already bad…  
> Watch the full episode on YouTube: **The Meeting That Could Have Been an Email**

Avoid vague calls to action such as “link in bio” without naming what the viewer will receive.

## 14. Quality checklist

Before approving content, ask:

- Does this feel specifically like Strawberry and Pineapple?
- Is the situation recognizable to an adult audience?
- Do both characters behave consistently with their personality?
- Is there a real interaction rather than two characters merely standing together?
- Is the humor kind rather than humiliating?
- Does the ending provide comfort, recognition, or a satisfying laugh?
- Can the idea be understood internationally?
- Does the Instagram version stand on its own?
- Is there a meaningful reason to continue to YouTube?
- Does the visual treatment match the canonical character references?

## 15. Maintenance

Update this document when a major creative decision is approved, including:

- Final account and character names
- Relationship or world changes
- New recurring locations or supporting characters
- Voice and language rules
- Visual prototype changes
- Platform strategy changes supported by actual performance data

Do not rewrite established personality traits merely to fit one isolated post. If repeated stories reveal a genuine improvement, document the reason and update the relevant section deliberately.
